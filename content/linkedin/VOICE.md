# How Camilla actually writes on LinkedIn

Built on 2026-09-02 from her five most recent LinkedIn posts. This is the
reference for the weekly drafting routine. Where this file and the generic voice
rules disagree, this file wins.

## The single biggest correction

The drafts written so far open with a **thesis about teams** and close with a
**soft pitch to HR**. That is a B2B consultant's voice. It is not hers.

Camilla opens with **something that actually happened to her**, or with an
admission. The insight arrives late, often understated, and frequently she does
not resolve it at all. Most of her posts sell nothing and ask for nothing.

Her real openers:

- "Over the weekend I completed my 6th ultra marathon. Here are a few reflections:"
- "I have to admit that I find it hard to meditate alone."
- "Yesterday A House joined me for a SUP yoga class Sickla sjö."
- "I still can't believe I get to call this work"
- "Yin-yang at work: is this something I need to change, or something I can accept?"

Note that not one of them opens with a claim about what teams get wrong.

## Rules taken from her actual writing

**Write from her own week, not from the market.** The subject is something she
did, noticed, or is currently struggling with. The lesson for the reader is
implied, not delivered.

**She is willing to be unresolved and unflattering about herself.** This is the
most distinctive thing in her writing and it is completely missing from the
drafts so far. Real example: "In some sessions I feel no sense of peace at all,
only how overwhelmingly busy my mind really is." Never smooth this out into a
tidy takeaway.

**Bulleted reflections are a signature.** A short setup, then "Here are a few
reflections:", then bullets using the • character, each two to four lines. Use
this structure for roughly one post in three.

**A question can be the whole frame.** She sometimes leads with the question
rather than saving it for the end: "is this something I need to change, or
something I can accept?"

**Real names and real numbers.** She names companies and partners she has worked
with (A House, Bruce Studios) and uses concrete figures ("44 participants, and
just six of them were women", "a 130 person waitlist"). Only ever use names and
numbers that are genuinely true and already public.

**She raises uncomfortable observations.** "Why is this such a male dominated
sport?" She is willing to point at something and not fix it.

**Emoji, sparingly and genuinely.** She uses them: ☯ 🙏 ☺️ 😱 🌿. Roughly one or
two per post, usually ending a line rather than decorating a heading. The
current no-emoji default is wrong. Never use 🚀 💡 ✅ or anything that reads as
engagement bait.

**Contractions are natural.** She writes "I still can't believe", "even on the
days when you don't feel like it", alongside plenty of "It is" and "I am". Do
not force either register.

**Short paragraphs, blank line between each.** Rarely more than three lines.

## Hard constraints

- **No em-dashes. No `&` (write "and").** Both still hold.
- **She has no corporate wellbeing clients yet.** NEVER write "the teams I work
  with", "my clients tell me", or any phrasing implying a book of business. This
  has already slipped into drafts and it is a fabrication. What she can truthfully
  reference: one-off sessions and events (A House, Bruce Studios), her teaching,
  her training, and her own practice.
- **No invented statistics or studies.**
- **No prices.** The site prices in SEK; point to /pricing at most.
- **The ultra running is real and it performs well, but at most one post in
  three may touch it**, and always as a lens on something else. She does not want
  to be boxed in as the running person.
- **Not every post sells.** At least one draft each week should ask for nothing
  at all. Her best-performing posts have no call to action whatsoever.

## Recurring themes that are genuinely hers

Meditation as something she practises and finds hard, not something she has
mastered. Habit and consistency over intensity. Pace you can sustain rather than
speed. Acceptance versus fixing. Getting people away from their desks. Women in
endurance sport. Gratitude for the work itself, expressed plainly.

## A note on her headline

Her LinkedIn headline currently reads "Workplace meditation and yoga for remote
and hybrid teams | Live online, and in person in Stockholm". The website says the
offering is fully remote and never in person. Worth her deciding which is true,
since drafts should match whichever she picks.
