# Camilla's LinkedIn voice and strategy

Built 2026-09-02 from her five most recent posts and their measured engagement,
then refined with her own format calls. This is the reference for the weekly
drafting routine. Where this file and any generic voice guidance disagree, this
file wins.

---

## 1. What LinkedIn is for

**LinkedIn demonstrates the business offering. Instagram carries the founder
story and day-in-the-life material.** Camilla set this split. Do not draft
day-in-the-life or personal-journey content for LinkedIn.

The problem it fixes: a stranger could read her recent posts and still not know
she runs weekly wellbeing sessions for teams, or how to book one.

**But most posts must NOT sell.** A feed that always asks for something stops
earning attention, and her social does better when she is not constantly
selling. The resolution is that her PROFILE carries the selling: her headline
already reads "Workplace meditation and yoga for remote and hybrid teams", so
anyone moved by a post is one click from the offer. That frees the posts to be
genuinely good.

## 2. What actually performed

| Post | Reactions | Comments | Words | Media |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6th ultra marathon, with reflections | **97** | **33** | 255 | image |
| "I still can't believe I get to call this work" | 56 | 2 | 69 | image |
| A House SUP yoga class | 23 | 0 | 60 | image |
| Yin-yang at work | 11 | 0 | 70 | image |
| "I find it hard to meditate alone" | 9 | 0 | 250 | text |

The obvious conclusion is the wrong one. **The ultra post did not win because it
was about running.** It won on form: long, personal, specific, carrying a real
figure ("44 participants, and just six of them were women"), and ending on a
question people wanted to argue with. Thirty-three comments against two is a
form effect, not a topic effect.

**Her two most workplace-relevant posts were her worst**, because they were
short, abstract and asked nothing sharp. **Her only text-only post came last.**

So: apply that form to work subjects, and always attach media.

## 3. Format

**No bullet points. Prose only.** Short standalone paragraphs of one or two
sentences with a blank line between them. That is how LinkedIn actually reads.
Never use the • character.

Worth noting honestly: her best post did use bullets, so the underlying
structure of a list still works. It just gets written as consecutive short
paragraphs instead of marked up as one.

**Always attach an image or b-roll.** Her only text-only post was her worst
performer by a distance.

**Length:** roughly 200 to 250 words for the opinion posts, shorter is fine for
the others.

## 3b. Positioning, and words to avoid

**She is BOTH a yoga teacher AND an ultra runner, and the combination is the
point.** Neither on its own is as interesting. Together they give her one
perspective worth listening to: how to chase a big goal in a way that lasts.
Never present the running as a separate hobby, and never cite the marathon count
as a credential.

**The frame is the balance of effort and ease, and listening to your body rather
than overriding it.** That is the sthira sukha idea and it is authentically hers
as a teacher. Do NOT frame it as endurance or limits: she explicitly rejected
"how far you can go without breaking" as too extreme, and it made her sound like
an endurance athlete rather than a yoga teacher. Avoid "push through", "grind",
"limits", "breaking point" and similar. It is worth asserting that ease is not a
soft idea, so the frame does not read as passive.

**Her strongest recurring theme**, in her own words: the irony of people working
on things that genuinely matter, often on sustainability itself, while working in
a way that is not remotely sustainable. Impact-driven people running themselves
into the ground. This names something her audience recognises in themselves, and
it is hers rather than generic wellbeing commentary.

**Say "rest", not "recovery" or "recharging".** She finds those jargonish. Plain
words throughout.

**Never argue against ambition.** Big goals are not the problem. The problem is
the assumption that the only route to them is going until something gives.

## 4. Do not lecture companies

Turn observations into questions she is asking herself and inviting the reader
to sit with, rather than statements about what workplaces get wrong. The
audience is HR, and telling them their own workplace is broken does not land.

The pattern that works: *is there anything in your week that is actually
restful, on purpose, and often enough to count? If there is, are you the first
person to drop it the moment things get busy? And is it treated as a nice extra,
or as part of how the work gets done?*

Same point, no lecture.

## 5. The weekly cycle

One post a week, rotating through four types. The routine derives the type from
`(ISO week + 1) mod 4`, so it advances on its own. Three of the four ask for
nothing.

**OPINION** — the winning form aimed at work. Opens with something that actually
happened to her, usually an admission. At least one concrete detail. Questions
turned on the reader. Ends on a real question. No ask. This is the comments post
and the most important one.

**SHOW THE WORK** — b-roll of a class or her own practice. What actually happens
in a meditation, breathing or chair yoga session. Soft close, no pitch.

**PRACTICAL** — something a manager or individual can use that same day. Three
breathing techniques before a hard meeting, a sixty-second desk reset. Written
to be saved. No ask.

**THE OFFER** — plainly: what she runs, who it is for, how to book. The ONLY
week with a hard call to action.

## 6. Video and music

**B-roll only, never talking to camera.** Camilla has ruled out talking-head
video and that is settled. Filmed class and practice footage is welcome and she
already has b-roll.

**Do not suggest a soundtrack.** LinkedIn has no licensed music library the way
Instagram does, so commercial music risks being muted or taken down and is not
licensed for that use. Natural sound or silence, with the text doing the work.
Most LinkedIn video is watched muted anyway.

## 7. How she actually writes

She opens with **something that actually happened to her**, or an admission,
never a thesis about teams. The insight arrives late and understated, and she
often does not resolve it.

Her real openers:

- "I have to admit that I find it hard to meditate alone."
- "Yesterday A House joined me for a SUP yoga class Sickla sjö."
- "I still can't believe I get to call this work"
- "Yin-yang at work: is this something I need to change, or something I can accept?"

Not one opens with a claim about what teams get wrong.

**She is willing to be unresolved and unflattering about herself.** This is the
most distinctive thing in her writing: *"In some sessions I feel no sense of
peace at all, only how overwhelmingly busy my mind really is."* Never smooth
that into a tidy takeaway.

**Real names and real numbers.** She names partners she genuinely worked with
(A House, Bruce Studios) and uses concrete figures. Only ever what is true.

**Emoji, sparingly and genuinely.** She uses ☯ 🙏 ☺️ 🌿, usually one or two,
ending a line. Never 🚀 💡 ✅ or anything that reads as engagement bait.

**Contractions are natural.** "I still can't believe" sits alongside plenty of
"It is" and "I am". Do not force either register.

## 8. Hard constraints

- **No em-dashes.** Commas or full stops.
- **No `&`.** Write "and".
- **She has no corporate clients yet.** NEVER write "the teams I work with", "my
  clients", or anything implying a book of business. This has already slipped
  into drafts once and it is a fabrication. She can truthfully reference
  one-off sessions and events she genuinely ran (A House, Bruce Studios), her
  studio teaching, her training, and her own practice.
- **No invented statistics or studies.**
- **No prices.** The site prices in SEK; point to /pricing at most.
- **Ultra running:** it is half of her positioning (see section 3b), so it may
  appear regularly as part of who she is and where her perspective comes from.
  But it is never the SUBJECT of a post: at most one post in four leans on it,
  and even then it must land on something about work. Never cite the marathon
  count as a credential.
- **No AI-tells:** no "in today's fast-paced world", "game-changer", "unlock",
  "dive in", "let's be honest", "the truth is".
- At most 0 to 3 hashtags.

## 9. A note on her headline

Her LinkedIn headline says "Live online, and in person in Stockholm", while the
website says the offering is fully remote and never in person. Worth her
deciding which is true, since drafts should match whichever she picks.
