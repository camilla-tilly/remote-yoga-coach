## Draft: effort and ease

I teach yoga and I run ultras. People assume the two are opposites. They are not.

Both come back to the same thing, which is the balance between effort and ease. Here are a few things I keep learning from doing both:

The hard part is rarely the effort. Most people I meet are very good at effort. The hard part is knowing when to ease off, and then actually doing it.

An ultra is not about being the fastest. It is about finding a pace you can hold. The person who finishes well is usually the one who did not slow down, not the one who went out hardest.

In yoga we practise noticing what the body is telling you instead of overriding it. It sounds simple. It takes years, and I am still learning it.

Here is what I notice in a lot of the people I teach. They are working on something that genuinely matters. Climate, cities, health, real change. And they will talk about sustainability all day while working in a way that is not sustainable at all.

I did the same thing for years. I thought rest was something you earned afterwards.

Rest is not the reward for the training. It is part of the training.

So the question I keep coming back to is a small one. Is there anything in your week that is genuinely restful, and does it stay there when things get busy?

That second half is the one that counts. Anything that only survives the quiet weeks was never really in place.
