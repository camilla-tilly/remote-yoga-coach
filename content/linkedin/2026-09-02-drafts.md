For years I only rested once I was already exhausted. That was the whole system. Push until something forced me to stop, collapse for two days, call it a break.

Ultra running is what changed my mind about that, which I did not expect.

In a training plan the rest is not what you get afterwards. It is scheduled in from the start, on set days, and the hard sessions are built around it. You do not earn it by suffering first. It is the thing that makes next week possible.

I now take a full rest day every week. I went years without taking one, and spent most of those years wondering why I felt stressed all the time.

I think a lot of us work the way I used to run. We go until we are depleted and then try to recover. Recovering from depleted takes far longer than staying topped up would have.

So what I keep wondering is whether rest could be preventative rather than a reaction.

Not a big gesture. Not a wellness day after a brutal quarter. Something small and regular that is already in the calendar before anyone needs it.

For me that is a morning meditation practice and one full day off running a week. Both are in the diary. Both are the first things I am tempted to drop the moment I get busy.

Do you have a way you actually build rest into your week?

# OPTION B: why groups work

The argument: people turn up for a group in a way they do not for an app or for
themselves. Set time, other people, someone holding it. This is the closest thing
to her actual differentiator, and it argues for it without pitching.

ATTACH THE GROUP FITNESS PHOTO from her early twenties.

---

In my early twenties I taught group fitness classes. This is me somewhere around then.

I have been thinking about why I still prefer teaching groups, and I think it comes down to something fairly unglamorous.

People turn up.

Not because anyone is more disciplined in a group. Because someone is expecting them, at a set time, and that turns out to matter far more than motivation does.

I notice the same thing in my own practice. I find it much easier to meditate when I know other people are sitting at the same time. On my own I can talk myself out of it in about four seconds.

There is something else too. In a group you still get your own experience, it is just held inside a shared one. Nobody is watching you. Everyone is slightly out of their comfort zone at the same time, which is oddly relaxing.

That is the bit I think gets lost with a wellbeing app. The content is usually fine. What is missing is the set time, the other people, and someone actually holding the space.

Fifteen years on from those classes, that has not changed at all.

What is the thing you always turn up to, and what makes it stick?
