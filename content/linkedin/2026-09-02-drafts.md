## Draft: recovery is a plan, not a reaction

I only ever used to rest once I had nothing left.

Not as a plan. As a collapse. I would push through a long stretch of work, reach the end of it, be useless for two days, and call that recovery.

Endurance training taught me that is not what recovery is. In a training plan the rest is written in first, at a fixed point in the week, and the hard sessions are built around it. You do not earn it by suffering. You take it so you can keep going.

I think a lot of us run our working lives the other way round.

So here are a few questions I have been asking myself lately, and you might find them worth sitting with too.

Is there actually anything in your week that helps you recover, regularly, and not just once things have already got bad?

If there is, are you the first person to drop it the moment work gets busy?

And where your team has something in place, is it treated as a nice extra, something fun and optional, or as a real part of how the work gets done?

Because waiting until you are depleted is not recovery. That is repair, and repair takes a lot longer.

What I keep coming back to is whether any of us have something genuinely non-negotiable in the busy season, rather than only in the quiet one.

What does that look like for you?
