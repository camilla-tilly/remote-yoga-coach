## Draft: recovery is a plan, not a reaction

I only ever used to rest once I had nothing left.

Not as a plan. As a collapse. I would push through a long stretch of work, reach the end of it, be useless for two days, and call that recovery.

Endurance training taught me that is not what recovery is.

In a training plan the rest is written in first. It sits at a fixed point in the week and the hard sessions are built around it. You do not earn it by suffering. You take it so you can keep going. Skip it and you do not get fitter, you get injured.

Almost every workplace I have been in does the opposite.

A few things I notice:

• Rest is treated as a reward. You switch off after the launch, after the quarter, after the busy stretch. There is always another busy stretch.

• The people who most need to stop are the least likely to book anything for themselves.

• Anything optional gets dropped first. If it is not in the calendar at a set time, with someone else holding it, it quietly disappears by week three.

• Waiting until you are depleted is not recovery. That is repair, and repair takes a lot longer.

I do not think most teams have a wellbeing problem. I think they have a scheduling problem wearing a wellbeing costume.

So, honestly: is recovery in your team's week on purpose, or is it just what happens after everyone burns out?
