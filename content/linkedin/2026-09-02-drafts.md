## Draft: the thing endurance training taught me that work gets backwards

I have run six ultra marathons. The part that surprised me had nothing to do with running.

It was how much of the plan is rest.

In a training block, recovery is not what you get after the hard sessions. It is scheduled first, at a fixed point in the week, and the hard sessions are built around it. Skip it and you do not get fitter, you get injured.

Almost every workplace I have been in does the exact opposite.

Here are a few things I notice:

• Rest is framed as a reward. You get to switch off after the launch, after the quarter, after the busy period. There is always another busy period.

• The people who most need to stop are the ones least likely to book anything for themselves. A wellbeing budget that relies on individuals opting in reaches the people who were already fine.

• Anything optional gets dropped first. If it is not in the calendar at a set time, with someone else holding it, it quietly disappears by week three.

• Consistency beats intensity, and it is not close. Ten minutes weekly does more than a half-day workshop once a year, in exactly the way four easy runs beat one heroic one.

I am not convinced most teams have a wellbeing problem. I think they have a scheduling problem wearing a wellbeing costume.

So, honestly: is rest actually in your team's week, or is it just something people are told they should be doing on their own time?
