## Draft 1: A practical tip for managers of remote teams

I keep hearing managers say their team is "fine" because nobody has complained.

Silence is not the same as wellbeing. On remote teams especially, people can be quietly burning out for months before anyone notices, because you don't see the tired face at the coffee machine anymore.

One small thing that helps: build a fixed 15 to 20 minute pause into the week that has nothing to do with output. Not a check-in, not a retro. A breathing session, a short chair yoga break, a guided moment of quiet. Something people can join with cameras off if they want.

It will not fix a burnout problem on its own. But it gives people a regular, low-pressure signal that rest is allowed, not something they have to justify or hide.

What does your team actually do to build in a real pause, not just talk about one?

## Draft 2: A short perspective on remote wellbeing

Most workplace wellbeing advice for remote teams is written as if everyone works the same way, in the same time zone, with the same energy at 2pm.

They do not. Some people need movement. Some need stillness. Some just need fifteen minutes where nobody is asking anything of them.

I think that is the real gap in a lot of wellbeing programmes. One yoga class, once, for everyone, checks a box. It rarely fits how different people actually recover.

What has worked better for the teams I work with is variety: a meditation session one week, a breathing class another, chair yoga when people have been sitting in back to back calls all day. Same coach, same trust built up over time, but the format flexes to what the team needs that month.

Curious how other people leaders think about this. Do you offer one format, or let it change?

## Draft 3: Behind the scenes — how the sessions actually work

A few people have asked me how the live sessions actually run, so here is the honest version.

Every team gets one flat monthly subscription and picks from three class types: a meditation class, a breathing class, or chair yoga. Teams often stick to one, but plenty alternate or mix all three depending on the week.

It is all live, over Teams or Zoom, with me as the coach every time. Camera is optional. Some people join fully off camera, and that is completely fine. Every session is recorded too, so anyone who could not make it live can still catch up in their own time.

Nothing fancy behind it. No app to download, no equipment needed for most sessions. Just a calendar invite and twenty to thirty minutes set aside.

If you have ever wondered what a session actually looks like, happy to answer questions here.
