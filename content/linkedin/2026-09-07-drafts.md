## Draft: why it is easier with other people

Why is it so much easier to keep a habit when other people are involved?

I've been curious about this since I started teaching group fitness at 23. This is one of the rooms I taught in.

Running is the clearest example for me. Alone in the dark in February, it's a negotiation. With someone waiting for me, it isn't. Same run, completely different odds.

Turns out it isn't just me. In 1898 a psychologist noticed cyclists rode faster racing another rider than they did alone against the clock, and went on to run what's often called the first experiment in social psychology. The effect got a name later: social facilitation.

But I don't think that's the whole story. There's also the fact that most of us will let ourselves down long before we'll let someone else down. Committing to other people is how a lot of us end up committing to ourselves.

And there's something simpler underneath all of it. In a group you don't have to decide anything. The time is set, someone else is leading, you just turn up.

That might be the real reason it works. Most habits don't die because we stop caring. They die because they need a decision every single time.

What have you only ever managed to keep up with other people?
