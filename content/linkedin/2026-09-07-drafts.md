- Social facilitation. Norman Triplett, 1898, noticed racing cyclists posted
  faster times against another rider than alone against the clock. Term coined
  by Floyd Allport, 1924. His original data was weak by modern standards.

Why is it so much easier to keep a habit going when other people are involved?

I started teaching group fitness at 23 (here's me teaching BodyPump back then). What struck me even then was who kept coming back, and who quietly stopped.

I think it comes down to three things.

You turn up for other people. Most of us keep a promise to someone else more easily than a promise to ourselves, so committing to a group ends up being a way of committing to yourself.

You don't have to decide anything. A workday involves hundreds, if not thousands, of small decisions, and decision fatigue is real. There is something genuinely lovely about turning up somewhere and being told what to do.

You're part of something shared. Everyone is doing the same thing at the same time, and that pulls you through the bits you'd have skipped alone. It stops being your discipline and becomes a shared experience.

It works the same whether it's a workout, a yoga class or a guided meditation. On your own it takes a decision every single time. In a group it's just what happens on a Tuesday.
