## Draft 1: Practical tip for HR / managers of remote teams

One thing I keep telling HR leads: skip the big one-off wellness day.

A two-hour workshop feels generous. It photographs well for the internal newsletter. But it barely moves how people actually feel three weeks later.

What does move the needle is smaller and more boring: the same 20 minutes, same time, every week. People stop deciding whether to show up and just show up.

That's the whole logic behind running sessions as a standing team habit instead of an event. Meditation one week, breathing practice the next, a chair yoga session when everyone's shoulders are wrecked from back to back calls. Small, repeated, easy to keep.

If you're planning wellbeing budget for next quarter, I'd rather see it go to four short sessions a month than one long one.

What's worked for your team, consistency or big moments?

## Draft 2: Perspective on remote wellbeing

Here's something I notice every week teaching remote teams: people's calendars have changed completely since 2020. Their bodies haven't caught up.

You can mute yourself, turn off your camera, mentally "leave" a meeting for a second. Your spine doesn't get that option. It's still in the same chair, same angle, hour four.

Remote work solved a lot of things. It didn't solve the part where humans need to move and breathe differently than they do when staring at a grid of faces. If anything, it removed the incidental movement office life used to give you almost by accident: the walk to a meeting room, the stairs, the different chair.

I don't think the answer is one big fix. It's small, regular resets built into the workday that don't require anyone to change clothes or leave their desk.

Curious how other people think about this one.

## Draft 3: Behind the scenes, how sessions actually work

A question I get from HR contacts before they sign up: what does a session actually look like?

So, briefly. Teams pick from three separate class types: guided meditation, breathing practice, or chair yoga. Some teams stick to one. Others rotate through all three depending on the week, or combine two in a single slot.

It all runs over Teams or Zoom, same coach every time, so there's continuity rather than a rotating cast. Camera is optional. Some people join with video on, plenty just listen with their eyes closed at their desk, and that's completely fine.

Every session gets recorded, so if someone's in back to back meetings at the actual time, they can do it later that day instead of missing it entirely.

It's one flat monthly subscription per team, so there's no per-head admin to manage.

Happy to answer questions if you're curious how it would fit your team.
