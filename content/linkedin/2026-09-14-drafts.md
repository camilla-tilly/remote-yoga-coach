## Draft 1: the two minutes before

MEDIA: a still, candid photo of Camilla sitting quietly before teaching,

Two minutes before I start teaching, I run through a short breathing reset. If I skip it, I can hear it in my own voice for the first ten minutes. A bit higher, a bit rushed.

I use different versions of it depending on how wound up I am. Here are three I'd actually reach for before something like a hard meeting:

1. Box breathing. In for four, hold for four, out for four, hold for four. Four rounds. This is the one for when it's your mind racing rather than your body, since counting gives it somewhere to go.

2. The double inhale. Two short breaths in through the nose, one long breath out through the mouth. The fastest one to feel, and the one I reach for when I've only got about ninety seconds.

3. Feet on the floor. No breath pattern at all, just both feet flat, and noticing the actual contact for five seconds. Sounds too simple to work. It works.

None of these make the meeting easier. They just mean you walk in as yourself, not mid-panic.

## Draft 2 (alternative, same type): the shoulders give it away first

By the third call in a row on a heavy day last week, I caught myself with my shoulders halfway to my ears. I hadn't noticed it happening, only that it had.

Here's the reset I use, and it takes about sixty seconds:

1. Interlace your fingers behind your back and roll your shoulders down, away from your ears, for three breaths.

2. Slow neck circles, one direction then the other, exactly as far as feels easy and not a bit further.

3. Reach both arms overhead, then let them drop, heavy, like you're dropping something you were holding.

None of it needs you to stand up or leave your desk. And honestly, it doesn't fix whatever put your shoulders there in the first place. It just means you show up to the next call without carrying the last one in your neck.
