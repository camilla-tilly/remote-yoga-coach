## Draft 1: Practical tip for HR and managers starting a wellbeing program

I get this question a lot from HR leads: how do you introduce wellbeing sessions to a remote team without it feeling forced.

My answer is always the same. Don't start with a full class.

Start with 15 minutes of breathing, on the calendar right before your heaviest meeting day. Not Monday morning as a nice gesture. The day people are already dreading.

Low barrier. No mat, no floor space, no changing clothes. Just a screen and a few minutes.

Once people feel the difference before a hard day, they usually ask for more themselves. That's when you introduce meditation or chair yoga as options, not requirements.

Programs that start big often fade fast. The ones that start small and useful tend to stick.

If you're weighing where to start with your team, what does your hardest day of the week actually look like?

## Draft 2: Short opinion piece on remote wellbeing

There's an assumption I run into often: remote and hybrid employees need less wellbeing support because they save time on commuting.

Having run sessions for these teams for a while now, I'd say it's often the opposite.

The commute used to be a boundary. A walk to the station, a drive home, something that marked the end of the workday. Take that away and the day just keeps going. Meetings back to back, then dinner, then a laptop open again at 9pm.

A short live session, meditation, breathing or chair yoga, does something an app on its own cannot. It puts a fixed point in the day that people show up for together, even with cameras off.

It's not a replacement for real boundaries. It's a start.

What does the end of the workday actually look like for your team right now?

## Draft 3: Behind the scenes, how a session actually works

People sometimes picture one long yoga class when they book a session for their team. That's not quite how it works.

I run three separate class types: meditation, breathing and chair yoga. Teams pick one, rotate between them, or mix all three depending on the week and what people need.

Camera is always optional. Some join with video on, most don't, and that's completely fine. This isn't a performance.

Every session is recorded, so anyone in a different time zone or stuck in a meeting can catch it later.

It's just me running these, live, over Teams or Zoom, for one flat monthly price per team regardless of headcount. No app to download, no new platform to learn. Just a calendar invite and 20 to 30 minutes back for your team each week.

Curious which of the three your team would try first?
